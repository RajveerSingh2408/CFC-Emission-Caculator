Learn the basics

CFCs are more than just a chemical label.

Their environmental significance comes from their ozone-depleting potential and historical use across refrigeration, aerosols and foams.

What are CFCs?

Chlorofluorocarbons are human-made compounds that were historically used in applications such as refrigeration, air-conditioning and aerosol systems.

  • They can contain chlorine, fluorine and carbon.
  • Certain CFCs have significant ozone-depleting potential.
  • CFCs are controlled under the Montreal Protocol.

CFC ≠ every refrigerant

A modern appliance having a refrigerant does not automatically mean that it contains CFCs.

  • R-600a is an isobutane refrigerant.
  • R-32 is a modern HFC refrigerant.
  • Exact refrigerant information is model-specific.
Environmental perspective

Benefits, trade-offs and responsibility.

✓ Positive direction

  • International controls have dramatically reduced CFC production.
  • Modern refrigeration technologies can avoid CFC refrigerants.
  • Better maintenance can reduce unnecessary refrigerant loss.
  • Responsible disposal supports refrigerant recovery.
  • Leak detection can reduce avoidable emissions.
  • Clear equipment labeling helps technicians identify the correct refrigerant.
  • Model-specific technical documentation reduces guesswork.
  • Efficient equipment can reduce energy demand during operation.
  • Professional servicing supports safer end-of-life handling.
  • International reporting makes long-term ozone-protection progress measurable.

× What still matters

  • Old equipment may contain legacy ozone-depleting substances.
  • Poor disposal can release refrigerants into the environment.
  • Refrigerant leakage can contribute to environmental impact.
  • Incorrect servicing can create avoidable emissions and equipment problems.
  • Some older equipment has limited technical documentation.
  • Refrigerant information can vary by model year and market.
  • Replacing equipment creates manufacturing and end-of-life impacts of its own.
  • Improper recovery can leave refrigerant in discarded equipment.
  • Reported production figures do not directly measure appliance leakage.
  • State-level CFC production figures are not provided by the UNEP country-level dataset.

Reduce unnecessary impact

Smarter equipment habits.

You do not need to modify refrigerant systems yourself. Good maintenance and responsible servicing are the safer approach.

01

Follow the manufacturer's schedule

Use the maintenance intervals recommended for your specific appliance or vehicle.

02

Keep airflow unobstructed

Clean accessible filters, vents and condenser areas according to the manufacturer's instructions.

03

Replace damaged seals

Worn refrigerator door seals can reduce efficiency and make the appliance work harder.

04

Do not self-service refrigerant

Refrigerant handling should be performed by an appropriately qualified technician.

05

Dispose responsibly

When replacing old cooling equipment, use an appropriate recycling or recovery channel rather than simply abandoning it.

06

Check the actual model

Never assume a refrigerant from a brand name alone. The exact model and technical documentation matter.
